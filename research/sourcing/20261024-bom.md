## Key findings

- **M18 over-discharge: assume the pack does not cut itself off behind a plain adapter.** Milwaukee lists "Discharge Protection" and per-cell monitoring as battery features but does not say the pack disconnects itself without a tool ([Milwaukee](https://www.milwaukeetool.com/products/details/m18-redlithium-compact-battery-two-pack/48-11-1811)). The Power Tool Institute says protection electronics are not all inside the pack and that an adapter "may not allow it to appropriately protect the battery during discharge" ([PTI](https://www.powertoolinstitute.com/?p=376)). Adapter makers warn about it or add their own cutoff ([PartsBuilt](https://www.partsbuilt.com/battery-adapter-with-low-voltage-protection-milwaukee-dewalt-compatible)). So the shared BOM keeps the XH-M609 cutoff, set to about 15.0-16.0 V (TBD from the cell datasheet). Its 20 A relay switches the main relay coil, not the actuator current.
- **M18 reference packs:** HIGH OUTPUT HD12.0 (48-11-1812): 18 V, 12.0 Ah; XC8.0 (48-11-1880): 18 V, 8.0 Ah. These figures come from retailer pages because the spec table on milwaukeetool.com did not load (the site lists both as no longer available). 216 Wh and 144 Wh are 18 V x Ah, not Milwaukee figures.
- **E-stop relay coil:** TEMCo 40 A relay with a 24 V coil. Whether it pulls in at 15 V is not published: bench-test before committing. A 12 V coil would be overdriven at 21 V (1.75x).
- **INA226 vs INA228:** the shared line is the Adafruit INA228 ($14.95, 85 V, verified). Generic INA226 boards are about $2-4 street price (no US vendor price confirmed) and use a 0.1 ohm shunt that reads only to about 0.8 A. Either part needs an external shunt to read the full actuator current.
- **STS3215 12 V variant:** C018 and C047 are both the 12 V, 30 kg·cm, 1:345 part; no Feetech page distinguishing them was found. The 7.4 V parts are C001 (1/345), C044 (1/191) and C046 (1/147).
- **AmazingHand** ([repo](https://github.com/pollen-robotics/AmazingHand), [Seeed wiki](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/hand_amazinghand/)): 8 Feetech SCS0009 per hand (4 fingers x 2 DOF, all in the palm), 400 g; Pollen's published BOM is EUR 116.89 / $135.59 for the mechanics, $148.80 with the Waveshare driver; Feetech SCS bus from a Waveshare driver (Python on a Pi) or an Arduino with the TTL Linker; external 5 V / 2 A per hand; software Apache 2.0, CAD CC BY 4.0; grip force not published. Seeed sells kits at $99 per hand (kit contents not listed by Seeed: confirm).
- **Hand comparison:** LEAP Hand, 16 Dynamixel XC330, about $1,797 per hand from ROBOTIS, CAD CC BY-NC-SA (non-commercial). InMoov hand, 5 tendon fingers with servos in the forearm, no current cost found. Inspire RH56DFX from about $5,783 per hand (reseller). **Recommended: AmazingHand.** It already has 4 fingers, keeps its actuators in the palm and costs about 1/18 of a LEAP hand.

## Alternates and risks

- **Price confidence:** Amazon, Home Depot, Walmart and Newegg hid prices from fetch, so most of their prices are search snippets (L). The DigiKey IC prices come from a DigiKey mirror. The concrete price is from a tracker last updated May 2025. Re-check before ordering.
- **Tier A servo rail:** the DS3235 is 5-7.4 V (32 kg·cm at 6 V, 35 at 7.4 V), so a 6.0-6.8 V rail gives about 32-33 kg·cm. Worst-case arm stall is about 19.4 A, so the BOM uses one DROK 10 A buck per arm rather than one 20 A module (generic 20 A modules are 15 A continuous), plus one for the head (its load is TBD).
- **Tier A springs:** the $4.99 Harbor Freight assortment has no published rates and is likely too light for 20-40 N. Size real springs once CAD gives arm mass and centre of mass.
- **Tier B 12 V bucks:** 2 EKYLIN 15-40 V to 12 V 20 A modules, one per arm, since all 14 servos stalling at once draw about 47 A. Whether they hold 12 V at 15 V input is unknown; this matters at the end of a pack. They also take 24 V, which suits tier C.
- **Ideal-diode modules:** no ready-made dual-input LM74700 or LM5050 board rated 20 A or more was found. The BOM uses single-channel 40 A modules (controller IC not named) with their outputs tied.
- **M18 adapter fuse:** its 30 A fuse is above its leads' 18-20 A rating. Fuse lower downstream.
- **Tier B extrusion:** 10 x 500 mm instead of 8 (a 350 x 250 x 450 box has 12 edges, about 4.2 m). Maker Store shows one price range per product, so the extrusion, T-nut and screw lines are marked E.
- **Tier C hand bus:** the Waveshare Bus Servo Adapter (A) page states 9-12.6 V input, which conflicts with 5 V SCS0009 use; Pollen's BOM uses a Waveshare driver at 5 V. Confirm before relying on it. Never share a bus with the 12 V STS servos.
- **Tier C power ICs:** the TPS25982 tops out at 24 V recommended (30 V absolute max), so use it on the 12 V branches only; TI points to the pin-compatible TPS25983 (26 V, 20 A, not priced). The JLCPCB $38 is an estimate (5 boards, economic assembly, about 120 joints and 5 extended parts) that excludes components, shipping and 2 oz copper.
- **Raspberry Pi 5 8 GB:** $175 at PiShop (it has risen from $80 at launch; $175 since April 2026); Adafruit shows $200. The Active Cooler is $10.95, not the old $5.
